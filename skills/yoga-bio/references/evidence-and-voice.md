# Evidence and voice workshop

## Build a private facts ledger

Separate completed training, current registration, in-progress training, workshops, retreats, practice years and teaching years. A 200-hour course plus an unfinished 300-hour course is not a completed 500-hour credential. A teacher attended on retreat is not automatically a certification lineage. A studio where someone practiced is not an employer. Documented instruction, mentorship, influence, employment and collaboration should be worded differently.

Record the user's preferred public name/pronouns, current teaching locations, intended audience and allowed personal details. Do not import a location or “currently training” claim from an old biography without checking it. If a credential is uncertain, omit it from ready-to-publish copy or leave an explicit placeholder in a draft. Explain the missing fact outside the bio, not as a vague claim inside it.

## Find recognizability

Ask for a few sentences in the teacher's own words, an actual class description, or cueing language they use. Listen for sentence length, humor, directness, imagery, and the kind of invitation they make. Distill the voice without copying a private story the user did not offer for publication.

Useful prompts, only when missing information blocks a good draft:

- What would a student notice in the first ten minutes?
- Where do you give people choice or permission to pause?
- What do you return to in your cueing?
- What belongs in this particular studio's description, and what can stay off the page?

Replace unsupported adjectives with observable teaching choices. “Inclusive” is not evidence of accessibility; a specific option or stated approach is more useful. Avoid promising healing, trauma resolution, or clinical outcomes from a yoga bio. Training in a modality does not confer medical credentials.

## Adapt to placement

| Placement | What earns its space |
| --- | --- |
| Schedule blurb | Class feel, relevant credibility, who should come |
| Website introduction | Approach, one meaningful origin beat, current practice and invitation |
| Long profile | Documented trajectory, influence, philosophy expressed through teaching, a chosen human detail |
| Workshop / retreat | The specific offering and qualifications relevant to it; do not recycle unrelated history |
| Social one-liner | A recognizable teaching focus and current public destination |

Use the requested length and point of view. Keep facts consistent across versions, but do not just truncate the long one. A beginning teacher can be concrete and compelling without inflating years or expertise.

## Final audit

Read each credential against the ledger. Verify dates/location claims are current. Count words. Check that the voice is consistent, the same person is recognizable across versions, and the invitation gives a prospective student a concrete sense of class. Keep assumptions and questions in a short separate editor's note. Do not publish or submit the bio without the user's instruction.
